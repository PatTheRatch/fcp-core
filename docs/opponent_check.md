# The opponent, drafted from the projections

Generated by `scripts/opponent_check.py` on the 2027 room (16 teams, $200, 13 places, 515 candidates from `BBM_Projections_2027_total.xls`), 2000 simulated drafts, board places jittered by 10, seed 0.

## Reading

**The exports this used were already the right ones.** The BBM settings
mistake of 2026-09-16 (projected games inflated about ten for the median
player) was caught and the files restored the same evening, before this check
first ran; only a quick roster test made in between used the inflated files.
Re-run 2026-09-17 on the confirmed exports, every table reproduces exactly,
and the roster test below replaces the quick one.

**The room's basis holds on the means.** Three ways of estimating what a
sixteen-team opponent posts in a week -- the one sixteen-team season brought
forward (what the room uses), the fourteen-team seasons scaled by the fitted
size effect (what it used while 2027 was fifteen teams), and a draft dealt
from the 2027 projections -- agree within 5% on points, rebounds, assists,
threes and turnovers, and within 10% on steals and blocks, which 2023 posted
low. The raw simulation sits 1-5% under the historical figures on points,
rebounds and assists, level to 5% under on threes and turnovers, and 5-10% over
on steals and blocks; on a games-actually-played basis it sits 1-7% over on
everything but turnovers, and 14-19% over on steals and blocks. The
historical basis is inside the bracket the simulation draws, so nothing here
says to change it, and the two independent routes to the number agree.

**The spread is the one place 2023 stands out.** Its between-team spread was
the widest on record (78 points a week against 52 and 36 in the two
fourteen-team seasons, 44-66 at other sizes), and the simulation at sixteen
teams gives 39. The room's opponent is
therefore wider than either the size-scaled alternative or the simulation
(PTS 122 against 103 and 100), which flatters a strong roster: the same
best-roster scores 5.52 expected wins on the room's basis and 5.32-5.35 on
the other two. A fifth of a category a week, all in the same direction.
Whether sixteen teams are genuinely more uneven or 2023 was, one season
cannot say.

**What the backtest can and cannot settle.** Simulated from the 2026 export,
2026's opponent came out 7-27% light in the counting categories. Most of that
is games: the export projects 56.7 games for the top of its board where the
top 182 scorers played 67.0, because a projection prices absence and a
manager streams round it. Put on the games basis the simulation reproduces
2026 within 4% in five of seven counting categories (blocks 9% high,
turnovers 7% low). The factor does not carry over cleanly, though. The 2026
export is not a preseason file -- its longest projected season is 68 games
where 2027's is 73, so it was pulled a couple of weeks in -- and part of its
1.18 is that, not streaming. The 2027 export's own factor is 1.09. Read the
raw and games-basis columns as the bracket, not either one as the answer.

**One thing the projections get wrong that the room inherits.** The
simulated pool shoots .489 from the field in both seasons; the league posted
.477 in 2026 and .462-.483 across its history. The room's opponent is read
from history (.479), but the roster's own percentage is read from the same
optimistic projections, so a roster projected to shoot .489 is credited a
64% chance of winning the category (z of 0.37 on a spread of .027) where a
roster shooting what the league actually shoots has 50%: about 0.15 of a
category a week, in every roster's favour. Free throws show no such gap. Not
changed here; recorded as the next thing to price.

## What is being compared

- **The room's basis:** `category_distributions` as the draft room uses it: 16 teams, seasons 2023, brought forward where the game drifts. Rates from the 3 most recent seasons.
- **Size-scaled alternative:** the nearest other size (14 teams, seasons 2024, 2026) scaled by the fitted per-team effect for the difference. This is what the room used while 2027 was fifteen teams.
- **Monte Carlo:** a draft dealt from the projections (method in the script's docstring), between-team spread from the simulation, within-team spread from history (seasons 2024, 2025, 2026).
- **Monte Carlo, on actual games:** the same, with counting categories scaled by 1.087: the top 208 scorers of seasons 2024, 2025, 2026 played 68.7 games on average, and the export projects 63.2 for the top 208 on its board. Streaming is what closes that gap in a real week.

The choice of basis does not change which roster is best, only the number the
best roster is credited with. Every roster is at its maximum on the diagonal
it was built for, so each basis does pick its own roster -- but the room's
2023-only roster is within 0.03 expected wins of the best score on all four
bases (5.53/5.53/5.38/5.17 against the 5.53/5.33/5.38/5.18 each basis' own
best manages). It is never worse than second, and it is the same roster as the
FG%-fixed room-basis optimum except for one end-of-bench dollar. The
size-scaled rosters are a different team -- six of thirteen players shared with
the room's, buying Cooper Flagg or Kevin Durant at the top -- and they win
nothing by being different: built for the size-scaled basis they score 5.33
there where the room's roster scores 5.32.

What moves is the level, not the choice. Raising the FG% bar .010 costs 0.15
expected wins on the room's own roster (5.53 to 5.38) and on the size-scaled
roster it buys (5.33 to 5.18), and it shifts the optimum at the top of the
roster, from a $49 guard to a $53 forward on the room's basis. Scoring the same
room roster against the size-scaled basis instead costs another 0.21 (5.53 to
5.32). Those are the shifts the categories section records, and they add up:
the difference between the most and least favourable reading of one fixed
roster is 0.36 of a category a week (5.53 on the room's basis, 5.17 once FG% is
lifted .010 and the opponent is read off the size-scaled basis too).

The practical reading: the basis is not what decides the roster, and the room's
2023-only basis is not leaving anything on the table by being narrower than the
alternatives. Buy the roster the room's own basis picks; the FG% correction is
worth more than the choice of basis and is the thing to fix first.
