Draft plan

The plan you take into the auction, made from the draft room's own arithmetic before the first pick (app/draft/plan.py): the league's spending shape as a ladder, what each man will probably go for, our ceiling for him from an empty room, and the model's lists. The model proposes; your figures, tags, ladder and notes are yours, kept beside the model's and never mixed into them. What you keep is what the draft room shows you on the night.

What he will probably go for: ESPN's average auction price blended half and half with our board, then sized to the room -- a quarter of the places at $1, 8% at $2, 98.8% of the budget spent. Tested on past drafts it misses by about $5 a man and $10 on the $40-and-up stars, where it runs about $3 low.

Our ceiling: the most he is worth to a roster that has bought nothing yet, the point where the best roster with him stops beating the best roster without him. It snaps to the ladder's places, so read it as the tier he qualifies for. On the night the room works it out again after every pick.

The lower of our ceiling and BBM's league value, never under $1. With no BBM value it is the ceiling.

Your going price replaces the model's as his cost in the best roster, and your ceiling caps what the plan will pay for him: a ceiling under his going price keeps him out of the best roster. The lists and the best roster are worked out again with them within seconds; the model's own figures are never overwritten, and with none of yours the plan is the model's exactly.

A man tagged must is in the roster whatever the model thinks: the plan is built again around him, bought at his going price (yours when you set one), with the ladder and the ceilings worked out for the places and money left. That build takes about two minutes; the page shows the last one meanwhile.

Nothing on this page bids, nominates or touches ESPN.

The ladder

How this league's best category teams spread their budget, most expensive man first (app/draft/shape.py): every drafted team-season's prices sorted and scaled to this budget, the top third of each season by category win rate, the mean at each place. Across 98 team-seasons the most balanced quartile beat the most top-heavy at every league size, so the shape is measured rather than the optimizer's own taste for stars and scrubs.

Balanced drafts won 53.1% of categories to top-heavy's 48.0% across 98 team-seasons, in 6 of 8 seasons. Top-heavy lost even when its stars stayed healthy.

$10-25 men average +2.6 a game in nine-category z-score; $1-2 picks average -0.6, close to a waiver pickup's -0.9, and half are dropped within a month. The wire cannot replace the middle tier.

Rebounds, blocks and FG% are worth real money: the wire adds almost none. Steals and threes are what cheap men and the wire supply.

This league pays about 1.5 times for stars, and the going price is sized to that. $101 is the most anyone has paid, so read the very top as a range.

While the top place is open one man may take the top place plus 10%: the room's own rule on the night (Allocation.cap). Your ladder moves the cap with it.

Yours when you change a place, kept only when the places add up to the budget with at least $1 each; Reset puts the league's shape back. The model's ceilings are worked out on the league's shape, whichever ladder you keep.

The board

Every man in the pool. The first view is the men the market or BBM prices at $3 or more, the ones the model worked a ceiling out for; Show the rest adds the others, with no ceiling.

Your going $ and your ceiling $ are yours: empty is the model's. Each is kept the moment you change it, and the lists and the best roster are worked out again with it within seconds (the stamp over the table says when). A ceiling runs from $1 to the cap; a going price from $1 to the most one man can cost.

Each category is the change in the chance of beating this league's median opponent when he takes the place of a par man: a roster of par men posts exactly the opponent's mean (app/draft/targets.py), a counting category shared evenly, a percentage at the opponent's rate on an average rostered man's attempts. The optimizer's own arithmetic, one man at a time. Green and red are what he does for a roster, not a grade.

A dot is a man in the best roster at your prices: the model's balanced build, or the same search again when you have figures of your own.

Your plan

This is what the draft room shows you on the night: your ceilings beside ours, your tags on the board.

First, at the price the plan buys them at, and the money and places left after them. What the lock costs is the best roster with them against the best roster without the requirement, in categories a week, for the set and for each man alone: the fan section's own arithmetic.

Your targets in ladder order, largest ceiling first: your ceiling where you set one, else the model's figure, and what they commit together against the budget.

The model's best roster at your prices, place by place against your ladder (the league's shape when you kept none).

The room reads these on the night and holds your ceiling up beside ours. Nothing bids, nominates or touches ESPN.
