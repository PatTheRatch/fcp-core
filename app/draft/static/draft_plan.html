Draft Plan

The game plan

  1. 1

    Draft balanced. Balanced drafts won 53.1% of categories to top-heavy's 48.0% across 98 team-seasons, in 6 of 8 seasons. Top-heavy lost even when its stars stayed healthy.

  2. 2

    Pay for the middle. $10–25 players produce about 28 a game; $1–2 picks produce 19, the same as a waiver pickup, and half are dropped within a month. The wire cannot replace the middle tier.

  3. 3

    Buy rebounds, blocks and FG% with real money. The waiver pool adds almost no blocks and hurts FG%. Leave steals and threes for the end — they are what cheap players and the wire actually supply.

  4. 4

    Take a star only inside the price. This league pays about 1.5× for stars, and the going price is sized to that: it prices the room's real $3,000 with a quarter of the places at $1. Wembanyama at $118 would be a league record ($101 is the most anyone has paid), so read the very top as a range.

  5. 5

    Your picks, your call. The room drafts like an average manager and has not beaten your drafts. Use these numbers to size bids, not to overrule a read you believe in.

What the model would build

Its best roster from an empty room, bought at going prices. Categories it wins under 25% of weeks are marked as conceded. Don't draft to this if it concedes categories. In this league's history, rosters built at the draft to punt a category won about 4% fewer categories than rosters of the same projected strength, and specialized teams won 12 points fewer of the close 5-4 weeks (docs/punt_builds.md). Read before bidding

  • Going price ranks players by ESPN's average auction price blended with our board, then shares the money this league actually spends: 24% of places at $1, 8% at $2, 98.8% of the budget used. Tested on past drafts it misses by about $5 a player, and $10 on $40+ stars, where it runs about $3 low.
  • Our ceiling is from an empty room and snaps to the spending ladder's places ($60, $46, $30, $16, $9). Read it as the tier a player qualifies for. On draft day the room recomputes it after every pick.
  • BBM is Basketball Monster's league value on total games, exported . Refresh the exports the week of the draft; injuries and roles move in preseason.
  • ESPN has not published 2027 projections yet. When it does, re-run the plan.
  • Category chips are league-standard values: green helps you win the category, red hurts.
  • "Check news" marks a player whose going price is far below our ceiling while BBM doesn't agree. Replaying 2022, the room's worst misses were exactly these — Kyrie Irving and Jonathan Isaac at $6 — cheap because the room knew something the projections didn't.
